---
name: Product scope
description: User-confirmed audience and core concept for UAE property matching.
---

The product serves all three journeys: rent a home, buy to live, and buy an investment. Do not narrow the first product to just one audience.

**Why:** When asked to pick the first audience, the user explicitly answered “ALL THREE.”

**How to apply:** Preserve all three journeys in designs, prototypes, and implementation scope unless the user changes the requirement.

The user's core concept is UAE real estate discovery that begins with the household's life: work, partner's work, school, worship, and sport, followed by financial goals and a top-three property shortlist.

**Why:** The user described life-first discovery as the central product idea, rather than conventional listing-first browsing.

**How to apply:** Keep household routines central to home-seeking flows, while avoiding irrelevant personal-location requirements for pure investment searches.

For property monitoring, the user chose clearly labelled sample data for historical prices and future scenarios.

**Why:** The user explicitly selected “Clearly labelled sample data” rather than connecting real market data.

**How to apply:** Do not imply monitoring uses verified transaction history or a live predictive model. Keep fictional history and assumption-based future scenarios clearly distinguished.

Monitor is available only in Buy to live and Invest, never in Rent. Clicking Monitor on a property should immediately open that property's monitoring screen, including when it is already monitored.

**Why:** The user explicitly restricted Monitor to the buying/investing workflows and asked for direct property-specific navigation.

**How to apply:** Hide both the Monitor navigation and property actions in Rent. Keep monitoring removal inside the dashboard rather than making a property-card click silently stop tracking.

Buy to Live and Invest must each have an independent Monitor, including tracked properties, selected property, viewing preferences and alert settings.

**Why:** The user explicitly said each workflow should have its own monitor with different selections, rather than sharing one global monitor.

**How to apply:** Changing or removing a property or alert in one journey must not alter the other. Buy to Live focuses on home value; investment monitoring also supports rental income.

The Monitor screen should be very simple and graph-first, with minimum/maximum alert controls and property metadata on the right, and historical information below.

**Why:** The user explicitly disliked the cluttered monitoring screen and said the graph should be the first thing users see.

**How to apply:** Keep secondary metrics, forecast controls, detailed tables and methodology from competing with the historical price graph.

The user wants the whole product to look sleek, simple, user-friendly and as intuitive as possible, consistently across all pages.

**Why:** The user explicitly extended the simplification request from Monitor to the entire product.

**How to apply:** Prefer clear primary actions and consistent navigation, typography and form controls over dense screens or decorative effects when adding future features.

Do not show a right-hand storyboard or summary box on any questionnaire page.

**Why:** The user explicitly said they do not want the storyboard and do not need that box at all, expanding the earlier landing-only removal to all steps.

**How to apply:** Keep progress and navigation above or below the questions rather than reintroducing a side summary panel.

Life entry should be input-first: simple work, partner and children/school inputs on the left, with a large map on the right that responds as places are selected.

**Why:** The user found the previous map-first guided picker unintuitive and explicitly requested this replacement. They later replaced the monochrome-map preference with a Google-like full-colour appearance.

**How to apply:** Keep household roles immediately visible, connect active inputs to map highlighting, and minimize steps to add a place. Do not restore the place-first/tag-later sequence. Preserve clear sample-location limitations until real place data is added.

Use a restrained blue-and-white theme for life-entry controls and pins, not a different accent colour for each household role. Default role order is My work, School, Partner work, Place of worship, Sports; users can rearrange it.

**Why:** The user explicitly rejected the many colours and asked to move these buttons to change their order.

**How to apply:** Distinguish roles through names, icons and numbers rather than rainbow colours. Preserve user-defined order when places are added or edited.

This is a prototype: a familiar Google-like map appearance is sufficient; a real Google Maps integration or API key is not required. Use a four-dot drag handle without visible up/down buttons or explanatory reorder instructions.

**Why:** The user clarified the prototype scope and explicitly asked for intuitive, customer-centred interactions rather than over-explaining controls.

**How to apply:** Keep visible controls minimal and self-explanatory, while retaining accessible labels and keyboard alternatives. Do not ask for Google credentials just to achieve this visual treatment.

The map must match the luxury blue-and-white look of the whole app, rather than use a separate sand/yellow regional palette.

**Why:** After trying the sea/sand/gold palette, the user said its colours clash with Realmax's “luxury, blue, white, posh” design and asked to sync the map with the app.

**How to apply:** Keep water, land, roads, map controls and pins in a coherent ocean-blue, pale-blue and white family. Avoid warm or rainbow accents on the map.

The Rent a Home journey includes a preference for paying rent upfront or splitting it monthly.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested this rent-specific preference.

**How to apply:** Keep the rent-payment schedule distinct from the deposit and fees budget. Do not imply a landlord supports a schedule unless its terms are actually known.
